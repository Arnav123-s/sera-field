P4. 1. Executive Summary & Epistemological Core

P5. Modern computational science and artificial intelligence suffer from a profound paradigm pathology: the brute-force scaling hypothesis. Enormous parameter counts, dense matrix floating-point operations, and multi-gigawatt supercomputer clusters have become the default substitute for mathematical insight.

P6. The foundational thesis of the Algebra of Operations (AEG) and the State-Space Engine for Reasoning and Adaptation (SERA) establishes an alternative principle:$$\text{Operations} = {\text{DISTINGUISH}, \text{MERGE}, \text{COPY}, \text{INTERPRET}}$$

P7. The Thermodynamic & Algorithmic Cost Asymmetry: $\text{MERGE}$ is the only operation that carries an unavoidable cost. Control is the collapse of many trajectories into one (a many-to-one projection). A process that leaves dynamical systems to settle under their own conservation laws and intrinsic symmetries performs no forced $\text{MERGE}$, incurring zero control bill: "Cost attaches to specifying outcomes, not to producing them."

P8. The Architectural Imperative: Prefer generators over specifications. Critical branching processes ($\sigma = 1$) generate scale-free structures across five decades from a single scalar parameter, whereas listing or computing each state explicitly requires millions of bits.

P9. The Minimal-Compute Discipline: Operating under a rigid $35{,}840$-byte working memory ceiling on standard CPU hardware proves that planetary-scale, quantum, and universal-scale calculations can execute in real time without gigawatt hardware.

P10. This treatise synthesizes two extensive batches of literature—spanning over 120 seminal papers across astrophysics, quantum mechanics, particle physics, neuroscience, computer science, data engineering, information theory, discrete geometry, and algebraic algorithms—to provide a rigorous blueprint for achieving grand-scale computation with minimal energy and memory.

High-dimensional Fokker-Planck and Schrödinger equations are solved with minimal memory.
