# Covariance-based compatibility revision, before finals

The energy-only qualification preserved vacuum answers but rejected too many weak new effects in two initializations: it failed its predeclared maximum 5% loss of new-context improvement. These failures, thresholds, data identities and checkpoints remain preserved. The original final cohorts are still unopened.

The diagnosed mechanism is that residual energy squares an effect and discards the direction of residual correlation. The context already contains signed velocity and force correlations. I therefore test one distinct, fixed alternative: center all three measured context statistics by their vacuum calibration mean, estimate their covariance, add 5% diagonal covariance shrinkage, and threshold the resulting squared Mahalanobis distance at the empirical 99.5th percentile. This uses evidence-conditioned covariance rather than a sweep over energy thresholds.

New calibration: 4,096 vacuum windows of six observations, seed 79092027. New development qualification: 1,024 mixed-context episodes, seed 89092026. These replace neither model-training data nor earlier qualification evidence. No field weights change. The new threshold, mean and precision matrix are recorded as acquired calibration state. The same qualification requirements apply: at least 50% new-context error reduction, at most 5% degradation relative to the ungated successor on new contexts, and vacuum MSE at most 1.25 times the retained predictor.

If a candidate fails these conditions it remains a candidate, not an approved default. Every original raw successor and both compatibility mechanisms receive an honest record. No final results are available to this decision.
