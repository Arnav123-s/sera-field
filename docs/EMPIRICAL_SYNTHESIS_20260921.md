## Adopt the useful addition: one relationship, coherent revision

The complete assessment must acquire one previously untaught instance of a
relationship and then reuse the **same unchanged acquired state and weights**
for forward prediction, inverse inference, changed conditions, constrained
planning and supported explanation. Record every query, predicted consequence,
source identity and actual intervention. There is no extra fitting for each view.
The language assessment must state whether its response is generated language,
a learned directional statement or an engineered rendering.

Then provide new independently checked evidence that conflicts with a prediction.
The learner must preserve the original goal and earlier evidence, qualify the
revision, return to the goal and re-evaluate all five views. Assess whether the
changes agree with the same revised situation, whether valid earlier regimes
remain usable and whether a new intervention actually occurred as requested.
Do not award five separate discovery credits for five views of one contribution.

Include a **single-observation condition** and a **targeted-acquisition condition**.
A single observation need not uniquely identify a new physical law. For example,
in F=q(v cross B), one known nonzero velocity leaves the component of B parallel
to v undetermined. B and B+lambda*v predict the same measured force. The correct
response preserves that equivalence class and investigates a discriminating
condition; the experiment must not teach or grade an arbitrary member as uniquely
established. A contradiction also need not erase a valid simpler regime.

This is a prospective addition to the whole-system curriculum/assessment. No
opened CONCEPT, NATIVE or JOINT final is reused for tuning. Exact sample counts,
source groups, conditions, rewards and selection rules will be frozen only after
the remaining complete-core engineering is qualified. Then the from-scratch
course, whole-system assessment and later incremental continuation follow in
that order, as requested.
